PLS-GS: Probabilistic Lidar Scaffolds for
Compact, Surface-Aligned Gaussian Splatting

1QUT Centre for Robotics   2Emesent

arXiv Code

PLS-GS produces compact, surface-aligned scaffolds that place primitives closer to the measured surface. From the LiDAR point cloud and SfM input we build a continuous 6D HGMM scaffold whose anchors are surface-aligned, in contrast to the unstructured anchor set of Scaffold-GS. PLS-GS renders at 35% of the model size of the LiDAR-initialized baseline, with higher precision and lower Chamfer distance.

Abstract

While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is typically optimized from images only, many robotics and mapping applications provide both camera and high-definition LiDAR data, combining rich visual appearance with accurate geometric structure. We introduce PLS-GS, a probabilistic LiDAR scaffold for Gaussian Splatting that exploits this complementary sensing setting to construct a compact 3DGS representation aligned to the measured surface. PLS-GS fits a Hierarchical Gaussian Mixture Model jointly over position and color, yielding a continuous and adaptive scaffold that eliminates the need for discrete scene partitioning such as voxel grids or octrees. The fitted mixture then guides the renderable 3DGS Gaussians: each rendering Gaussian is associated with a GMM component and constrained in the local surface frame induced by that component's covariance. A Mahalanobis support loss bounds the deviation of the rendering Gaussians from the corresponding LiDAR-derived surface. Across five large outdoor scenes, PLS-GS places primitives markedly closer to the LiDAR measurements than both image- and LiDAR-initialized baselines, while employing substantially fewer primitives, yielding a considerably more compact model and faster rendering. At matched capacity, PLS-GS places primitives closer to the LiDAR measurements, with comparable rendering quality.

Method

Surface-aligned anchors. Scaffold-GS (left) anchors neural Gaussians at a point with world-axis offsets; PLS-GS (right) anchors them in the mixture component's eigenvector frame, aligning their spread with the local surface. A Mahalanobis support loss keeps them close to it.

HGMM-based anchor growth. Neural Gaussians spawned in regions left uncovered by the scaffold accumulate high photometric residual (blue). A local HGMM is fitted to them and its leaf components become new anchors (green), so uncovered regions shrink progressively over training.
